# Example Ladder: Generic Individual Contributor (5 levels)

**This is an example, not a standard.** It is a short, generic ladder written for this skill so it can run when a user has no framework of their own. It is not taken from any company. Real ladders are longer, role-specific, and calibrated by the people who use them. Any rating made against this ladder must be labeled as such.

The ladder is role-neutral: "craft" means the core skill of the role (code, design, analysis, writing, and so on).

## Dimensions

- **Scope:** how large and how well-defined the work is.
- **Impact:** what changed because of the work, for users, the team, or the business.
- **Craft:** depth and quality in the role's core skill.
- **Execution:** planning, reliability, and finishing.
- **Influence:** how the person affects others: collaboration, communication, and raising the people around them.

## Level 1: Early career

- **Scope:** Completes clearly specified tasks. Learns the team's tools and conventions.
- **Impact:** Work ships and does what was asked. Can explain who the task is for.
- **Craft:** Produces correct work with regular review. Applies feedback and does not repeat the same mistake.
- **Execution:** Gives honest status. Raises blockers early instead of waiting.
- **Influence:** Works well inside the immediate team. Asks questions openly.

## Level 2: Independent contributor

- **Scope:** Owns features or work items from start to finish with light guidance. Turns a loosely stated request into a concrete plan.
- **Impact:** Chooses how to do the work with its purpose in mind. Can name the result it produced.
- **Craft:** Produces solid work that needs little rework. Reviews peers' work usefully.
- **Execution:** Estimates own work reasonably and hits most commitments. Spots risks inside own area.
- **Influence:** Helps newer teammates. Writes clearly enough that others can pick up the work.

## Level 3: Senior

- **Scope:** Owns a problem area, not just tasks in it. Works through real ambiguity and decides what to build, not only how.
- **Impact:** Delivers results the team or its customers can measure. Says no to low-value work and explains why.
- **Craft:** Others seek them out as the expert in at least one area. Makes trade-offs others can follow and defend.
- **Execution:** Plans multi-person or multi-week efforts. Delivers predictably and adjusts the plan when facts change.
- **Influence:** Mentors others in a way that shows in their work. Works across team lines to unblock shared goals.

## Level 4: Staff

- **Scope:** Sets direction for a set of related problems across several teams. Finds the problems nobody has assigned.
- **Impact:** Moves outcomes at the organization level. The organization's results would be measurably worse without this work.
- **Craft:** Sets quality standards and patterns that other teams adopt.
- **Execution:** Leads large efforts through others. Fixes the root causes of recurring obstacles, not just single instances.
- **Influence:** Builds agreement among people who don't report to them. Grows senior contributors.

## Level 5: Principal

- **Scope:** Shapes multi-year direction for a major part of the company.
- **Impact:** Work changes company strategy or results, and leadership seeks it out.
- **Craft:** Recognized expert inside and often outside the company. Their judgment is trusted on the hardest calls.
- **Execution:** Turns long-range goals into programs that many teams carry out.
- **Influence:** Shapes culture and how decisions get made. Develops the next generation of senior leaders.

## Using this ladder

- Rate only against the current level and the one above it.
- Most people show uneven strength across dimensions. Uneven strength is normal, not a gap.
- A level's description is a pattern of sustained evidence, not a single impressive project.
